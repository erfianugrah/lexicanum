This page lists which service to use for each part of a keyboard and a mouse, ordering from Singapore in quantities of one to five. The keyboard parts are those of the GRIDDLE LX ([series hub](/reference/griddle-lx-hall-effect-keyboard/)); the mouse parts are a thin-walled shell of about 120 x 65 x 40 mm and a dense board of about 30 x 60 mm. How to place the JLC order is in [Ordering a 4-layer keyboard PCBA (printed circuit board assembly) and plate at JLCPCB](/guides/jlc-4-layer-pcba-keyboard-order/), and the case design is in [Designing the GRIDDLE LX keyboard case in build123d](/guides/keyboard-case-build123d-dfm/).

Prices were read on 2026-11-24. Measured prices are anonymous instant quotes or a real order; published prices come from the vendor's page.

## Which service for which part

| Part | Use | Worth one competing quote | Known cost, 2026-11-24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keyboard main board, assembled | JLCPCB, Economic assembly when every part is on one side[^jlc-pcba-cap] | PCBWay[^pcbway-cap], PCBgogo[^pcbgogo-cap], Seeed Fusion[^seeed]; Wizlogix in Singapore[^wizlogix] | Rev 1 board with its FR4 (flame retardant 4) plate: US\$146.41 (measured) |
| FR4 switch plate | JLCPCB, on the same order as the board | Any PCB fab | Supplement after review, US\$22.11 on rev 1 (measured)[^jlc-extra] |
| 1.5 mm aluminium, POM (polyoxymethylene) or PC (polycarbonate) switch plate | PantheonKeys, Singapore[^pantheon] | JLCCNC[^jlccnc-guide] | PantheonKeys: POM S\$35, PC S\$35, aluminium S\$49 (published)[^pantheon-js]. JLCCNC: aluminium US\$37.57, POM US\$57.04 (measured) |
| Case tray, printed | ZELTA3D[^zelta-mjf] or Additive Inn[^additive-mjf] in Singapore, after a flatness test print | JLC3DP | No price without an uploaded file |
| Case tray, machined | JLCCNC | Xometry Singapore[^xometry-sg] | US\$109.20 for the anodised CNC (computer numerical control) case, 6-day build (measured) |
| Mouse shell | MJF PA12 at ZELTA3D, one shell first | JLC3DP as the cheap comparison; SLA (stereolithography) resin at ZELTA3D[^zelta-sla] for thicker walls | No price without an uploaded file |
| Mouse board with a PixArt sensor | JLCPCB, with the sensor consigned or hand-soldered[^jlc-consign] | AISLER, which sources hard-to-find parts[^aisler] | Consignment US\$70 per 50 part numbers plus US\$15 handling (published) |

## PCBs and assembly

JLCPCB is the default for assembled boards. Economic assembly takes a single board from 10 x 10 to 470 x 500 mm with parts on one side; Standard takes both sides, from 70 x 70 mm[^jlc-pcba-cap]. It accepts consigned parts, which is the route for a sensor it does not stock[^jlc-consign]. Its help pages list the surcharges for small boards and for slot-heavy plates[^jlc-extra].

The other assemblers are worth a quote:

- PCBWay: boards up to 250 x 500 mm, with an option for bottom-side assembly[^pcbway-cap]. It also charges extra for boards with many milled slots, so it does not avoid the plate supplement[^pcbway-extra].
- PCBgogo: from one piece, boards up to 400 x 1200 mm[^pcbgogo-cap].
- Seeed Fusion: assembly from one piece[^seeed].
- AISLER, Germany: one board, and it sources parts it cannot find in stock[^aisler].
- Singapore: Wizlogix, MPN Tech, Statron and TC Precision quote by form. Wizlogix's 50 x 50 mm minimum puts a mouse board on a panel[^wizlogix].

## Plates and machined parts

| Service | Use for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| PantheonKeys, Singapore | 1.5 mm plates in aluminium, POM, PC, FR-4 (flame retardant) or brass | Flat price per material, 7 to 28 business days, pickup. Its page gives no thickness, tolerance or corner radius, so ask before ordering[^pantheon] |
| JLCCNC | Plates, trays, small metal parts | Instant quotes. Milled inside corners are R0.5 at the smallest[^jlccnc-guide]; laser cutting holds 0.1 mm[^jlccnc-sheet], so cut a tester strip first[^plate-testers] |
| Xometry Singapore | Machined trays from a local marketplace | Needs an account to quote; the shop that does the work is not named[^xometry-sg] |

## 3D printing

| Service | Processes | Lead time | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZELTA3D, Singapore | HP MJF, SLA, SLS (selective laser sintering), FDM | 4 to 6 working days for MJF | First choice for shells and trays in Singapore; recommends 2 mm walls for parts of 10 to 30 cm[^zelta-mjf] |
| Additive Inn, Singapore | MJF, SLA, SLS, CNC, vacuum casting | 12 to 15 working days for MJF | Larger MJF bed than ZELTA3D[^additive-mjf] |
| JLC3DP | MJF, SLA, SLS, FDM | Ships with a JLCPCB order | Cheapest, but quality varies between batches. Its insert service covers M3 to M5, not M2[^jlc3dp-m3] |
| NLB MakeIT | Bambu P1S, PLA | Book a slot | Free; for fit prints only[^nlb] |

Long, flat printed parts warp at every bureau read; JLC3DP publishes 0.4 to 0.5 mm on flat plates over 150 x 150 mm[^jlc3dp-tol]. Order one ribbed test print of the tray from two bureaus before the real part, and machine the tray if both warp. For mouse shells, people who ship MJF-printed mice report wide quality differences between MJF (Multi Jet Fusion) bureaus, so order one shell before several.

## Ruled out

| Service | Why |
|---|---|
| SendCutSend | Ships to the United States and Canada only (third-party summary)[^sendcutsend] |
| OSH (open-source hardware) Park | No assembly, and about US\$348 for three bare boards of the keyboard's size[^oshpark] |
| Shapeways | Bankrupt in 2024 and relaunched; no reason to pick it over the Singapore bureaus[^shapeways] |
| Craftcloud, Xometry for printing | The printing partner is not named, so quality varies per order; a hobbyist who used them rated them mid |
| Singapore makerspaces for plates | The laser cutters there cut wood and acrylic, not aluminium[^makerspaces] |
