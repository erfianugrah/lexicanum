This page answers one question for each part of a keyboard and a mouse: which fab service, and what to check before paying. It is written from Singapore, for one-off to five-off orders, and the keyboard parts are those of the GRIDDLE LX ([series hub](/reference/griddle-lx-hall-effect-keyboard/)); the mouse parts are generic: a thin-walled shell of about 120 x 65 x 40 mm and a dense board of about 30 x 60 mm. The order mechanics at JLCPCB are in [Ordering a 4-layer keyboard PCBA and plate at JLCPCB](/guides/jlc-4-layer-pcba-keyboard-order/); the case geometry and its printability gate are in [Designing the GRIDDLE LX keyboard case in build123d](/guides/keyboard-case-build123d-dfm/). Neither is repeated here.

## TL;DR

- Boards: JLC Economic assembly takes a single board from 10 x 10 to 470 x 500 mm, single-sided only, 2 to 50 pieces. A sensor JLC cannot stock goes in as a consigned part (US\$70 per 50 part numbers plus US\$15 handling) or is hand-soldered.
- Switch plate: FR4 rides on the JLC PCB order but is billed a slot supplement after review (US\$22.11 on rev 1). A 1.5 mm Al, POM or PC plate is a Singapore shop at flat prices of S\$35 to S\$49 if it answers four questions, otherwise JLCCNC at US\$37.57 to US\$70.63.
- Case tray: every bureau read publishes about 0.3 % for MJF and SLS, which is 0.5 to 0.7 mm over 230 mm, and every one admits that large thin flat parts warp. Print a ribbed flatness coupon at two bureaus first; CNC is the fallback at US\$109.20.
- Mouse shell: practitioners who ship MJF mice use MJF PA12 with a removable stiffening cage. Walls of 1 to 1.5 mm are at or below every published MJF minimum, so one test print at one bureau decides.
- Buying from Singapore: goods plus shipping above S\$400 pay 9 % GST on the whole value. The carrier you pick moves JLC shipping more than anything else: US\$7.60 to US\$35.42 on one cart.

The same tree as a list:

1. PCB with parts:
   1. Parts on one side: JLC Economic assembly, 10 x 10 to 470 x 500 mm, 2 to 50 pieces, HASL and not OSP.
   2. Parts on both sides: JLC Standard, 70 x 70 mm minimum, so small boards need rails or a panel.
   3. A part JLC does not stock (a PixArt sensor): consign it, or hand-solder it after assembly.
   4. A side of 3 cm or less: expect the small-board surcharge; panelise if you order more than 30.
2. Switch plate:
   1. FR4 at 1.6 mm is acceptable: add it to the JLC PCB order and budget the slot supplement.
   2. 1.5 mm Al, POM or PC: ask PantheonKeys the four questions below, else JLCCNC with the 0.05 mm option and relief cuts in the corners. Cut a tester strip first if a laser cuts it.
3. Case tray: print a ribbed flatness coupon at two bureaus. Warp of 0.5 mm or less at one of them: print the tray there. Both warp: CNC at JLCCNC, or a routed sandwich of layers.
4. Mouse shell: MJF PA12 with a stiffening cage cut off afterwards, one shell at one tested bureau first. Walls well above 1 mm and a smooth finish wanted: SLA tough resin.
5. Small metal part (weight, wheel, frame): JLCCNC.
6. Fit check only: FDM at NLB MakeIT or your own printer.
7. Any overseas order: goods plus shipping over S\$400 is charged 9 % GST on the whole value unless the vendor charged it at checkout. Screws and inserts from JLCMC are a separate order.

## What JLC assembly takes

JLC Economic takes a single board from 10 x 10 to 470 x 500 mm (a panel from 10 x 10 to 250 x 250 mm), single-sided only, 2, 4 or 6 layers, 0.8 to 1.6 mm, 2 to 50 pieces, standard stackup, minimum package 0402, minimum IC pitch 0.4 mm, minimum BGA pitch 0.5 mm, build time 1 to 3 days (published)[^jlc-pcba-cap]. Standard takes both sides and a single board from 70 x 70 to 460 x 500 mm, 2 to 80,000 pieces, packages down to 01005, IC pitch 0.35 mm, BGA pitch 0.3 mm, build time of 4 days or more (published)[^jlc-pcba-cap].

A figure of 30 x 90 mm as an Economic size limit appears on none of the JLC pages read. The numbers near 30 that do exist are different limits:

| Figure | What it limits | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Side of 3 cm or less | A PCB-fab surcharge when the delivery format is Single PCB; the fee table is an image and the amounts were not read | JLC extra-charge page, item 10[^jlc-extra] |
| 2 to 30 pieces | The Economic order cap for 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2 mm boards (1.6 mm boards go to 50) | JLC capabilities, specification table[^jlc-pcba-cap] |
| More than 30 boards | Economic needs panelisation, panel up to 250 x 250 mm | JLC assembly FAQ, item 10[^jlc-faq] |

Fees as published for each tier (US dollars)[^jlc-price]:

| Fee | Economic | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | 8.18 | 25.56 single-sided, 51.12 double-sided |
| Stencil | 1.53 | 8.21 single-sided, 16.42 double-sided |
| Per unique part loaded | 3.07 for Extended parts; Basic and Preferred Extended are free | 1.53 for all parts |
| Handling | none listed | 14.93 minimum |

JLC's own cost guide adds that Standard is never cheaper than Economic on the same design, because of the handling floor, and that Economic has no V-cut panels (mouse-bite tabs are allowed)[^jlc-reduce]. Depanelling on Economic leaves about 0.2 mm of tolerance, so keep copper and parts more than 0.3 mm from the edge[^jlc-faq2].

### Consigned parts

JLC accepts consigned parts. The overseas service fee is US\$70 per 50 part numbers plus US\$15 handling, so US\$85 for 1 to 50 lines; loose parts cost US\$0.142 per piece plus a manual fee; there is no incoming test; you pay taxes and duties; and consigned parts ship separately and cannot ride with the PCBs[^jlc-consign]. JLC only assembles boards it fabricates[^jlc-faq].

This is the route for a PixArt sensor. On 2026-11-24 the LCSC listing for the PAW3395DM-T6QU read "Out of Stock" (a search snippet; stock changes daily)[^paw3395] and the PMW3360DM-T2QU showed 0 in stock at JLC[^pmw3360]. JLC will assemble a part number it lists, but out of stock means global sourcing or consignment. Check live stock before the design freezes.

## Switch plates

An FR4 plate on the JLC order has a known surcharge and a known thickness problem. The surcharge structure and the supplement arithmetic are in the [JLC order guide](/guides/jlc-4-layer-pcba-keyboard-order/#step-7-the-plate-as-a-zero-copper-pcb); the published rule is US\$16.41 plus US\$49.25 per m2, and a separate routing fee for slot-heavy boards (slots 0.8 to 1.0 mm wide at 80 m of path per m2 or more, wider slots at 120 m per m2 or more)[^jlc-extra]. The plate PCB is 1.6 mm. The Cherry panel specification is 1.5 mm with a tolerance of 0.1 mm (recorded in the project's case notes; no datasheet link was kept), so 1.6 mm sits at the top of that window. Whether JLC offers 1.5 mm for a plain PCB was not checked.

A cutout is 14.0 mm square with a tolerance of 0.05 mm, and a milled inside corner takes the radius of the cutter. That sets the choice between the routes for a 1.5 mm plate.

| Route | Material and price | Tolerance and corners | Lead and shipping |
|---|---|---|---|
| PantheonKeys, Singapore | POM S\$35, PC S\$35, FR-4 S\$45, Al 5052 or 6063 S\$49, brass S\$64 (published, flat per variant)[^pantheon-js] | Not stated | 7 to 28 business days, pickup at the shop[^pantheon] |
| JLCCNC milling | 230.6 x 97.25 x 1.5 mm Al 6061: US\$37.57 at ISO 2768-m, US\$45.11 at 0.05 mm, US\$75.33 at 0.02 mm. POM: US\$57.04, US\$70.63 at 0.05 mm (measured) | Minimum inside radius R0.5 at depths up to 3 mm[^jlccnc-guide] | UPS Express Saver US\$9.43, FedEx US\$16.81, DHL US\$28.98, economy line US\$4.19 (measured, Singapore address) |
| JLCCNC laser | Not auto-quoted: a 60-cutout plate returned a manual quote | Cutting tolerance 0.1 mm[^jlccnc-sheet] | Same carriers |

The two quotes above are for the GRIDDLE LX plate: 230.6 x 97.25 x 1.5 mm, 60 square cutouts, five 2.2 mm holes. All prices are anonymous instant quotes without coupons.

Three checks before ordering:

1. Corners. R0.5 is what JLCCNC mills, and R0.3 or sharp needs wire EDM (not quoted) or relief cuts in the DXF[^jlccnc-guide][^jlccnc-edm]. Whether an MX switch seats in a 14.0 mm cutout with R0.5 corners is not verified. The JLCCNC page priced the sharp-cornered file instantly, which says nothing about manual review.
2. Laser. JLCCNC's laser holds 0.1 mm[^jlccnc-sheet] and RapidDirect puts its standard at 0.10 mm and its 0.05 mm class on high-end fiber lasers only[^rapiddirect], both outside the 0.05 mm window. Cut a tester strip first: the public plate-tester project steps the cutout from 13.65 to 14.05 mm in 0.05 mm increments[^plate-testers].
3. PantheonKeys states no thickness, size limit, tolerance or corner radius. Ask four things: is 1.5 mm Al and POM stocked, is 230 x 97 mm acceptable, what is the cutout tolerance and corner radius, and is the price per plate for a 60-cutout ortho layout.

Two more options fail for different reasons. SendCutSend ships to the United States and Canada only, per a third-party summary that was not checked on its own site[^sendcutsend]. Singapore makerspaces are no help for metal: NLB MakeIT has an xTool diode-class laser that cannot cut aluminium[^makerspaces].

## The case tray: warp is process-wide

Published tolerance for MJF and SLS at every bureau read is about 0.3 %, which is 0.7 mm over 240 mm, and ZELTA3D claims 0.2 %, about 0.5 mm[^zelta-mjf]. The warp figures are worse than the tolerances suggest:

| Source | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| JLC3DP's MJF tolerance blog | Warping on large flat surfaces is "unavoidable"; edge warp of 0.4 to 0.5 mm on flat plates over 150 x 150 mm; about 20 % of large flat MJF parts warp measurably when thermal balance is not managed[^jlc3dp-tol] | Published, partly marketing |
| JLC3DP order-time warning on a keyboard case | "High risk of deformation" for flat parts, frame parts and enclosures without strong ribs[^jlc3dp-warn] | Anecdotal (one forum post, only the original text read) |
| ZELTA3D | "A risk of warpage" for large flat unsupported planes; add ribs; recommended wall 2 mm for 10 to 30 cm parts[^zelta-mjf] | Published |
| A US bureau's blog | Ribs go underneath, not on the large face; glass-filled nylon is a better start; a plate that must be truly flat may be a machining job[^digiforge] | Published |

So another bureau moves the risk and does not remove it. The approach that does not depend on a vendor's claim is a flatness coupon: a ribbed 240 x 105 mm part with a floor of at least 2 mm, printed at two bureaus before the real tray and measured flat on a surface plate or a pane of glass. The pass line, 0.5 mm of warp, is a design choice and not a published limit. If both coupons warp past it, the tray goes to CNC or to a routed sandwich of layers.

Where the tray can go:

| Option | Fit and constraint | Cost and lead |
|---|---|---|
| ZELTA3D MJF, Singapore | Bed 380 x 280 x 380 mm; 4 to 6 working days (2 to 3 expedited)[^zelta-mjf] | No price without an upload |
| Additive Inn MJF, Singapore | Up to 670 x 360 x 550 mm; about 12 to 15 working days[^additive-mjf] | Ballpark S\$0.50 to S\$1.50 per gram for SLS and S\$0.30 to S\$0.80 for SLA on its pricing page[^additive-price]; no MJF price |
| JLC3DP | Inserts only in M3 to M5, so M2 bosses need your own; parts of 250 mm and longer carry a surcharge of at least US\$8 and 1 to 2 more business days[^jlc3dp-insert][^jlc3dp-surcharge] | The cheap control, from abroad |
| JLCCNC milling | The CNC variant of the case, 267.6 x 134.25 x 13.86 mm, Al 6061 bead-blasted and anodised, ISO 2768-m | US\$109.20 for a 6-day build; US\$98.01 at 11 days, US\$146.52 at 3 days (measured, quote only) |

A resin printer of the 200 x 125 mm class cannot lay a 240 mm tray flat, and the large-format Form 4L costs US\$19,024 before tax[^form4]. How a thin-walled print goes wrong at a vendor, and the review record of a resin fit print, are in [Part 5 of the case guide](/guides/keyboard-case-build123d-dfm/#part-5-the-printability-gate).

## Mouse shells

The published evidence and the practitioner evidence point in different directions, so they are listed separately.

### Published

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| Formlabs case study of a mouse-mod maker[^zeromouse] | The mod kit was prototyped and produced on outsourced HP MJF nylon 12, and a 58 g mouse became 29 g. He names MJF warping and small details that "would not hold up", and an order minimum of 1,100 pieces at his vendor (that is one vendor, not MJF in general). His later shell is 21 g in Black Resin on a Form 4L. Vendor-written, so read it as a maker quote relayed by a vendor |
| A 2020 forum post on an MJF-printed mouse[^mousereview] | 44 g without feet, with a button-cell battery. Powder was trapped in a side-button pocket and had to be cleaned out and the pocket deepened; screw holes came out slightly large for M1.4 screws |
| ZELTA3D MJF page[^zelta-mjf] | Recommended wall 1 mm under 10 cm and 2 mm for 10 to 30 cm; a 120 mm shell sits in the 2 mm band |
| JLC3DP MJF blog[^jlc3dp-tol] | Walls under 1.5 mm hold only to 0.3 mm or worse; through-holes print 0.2 to 0.3 mm small; snap features have low repeatability; recommends 1.5 to 2 mm walls |

A shell of about 120 x 65 x 40 mm with 1 to 1.5 mm walls therefore sits at or below every published MJF minimum. On those minimums alone, SLA is the only process with published evidence of thin shells that work. That evidence is the maker's own account of his shell, and it does not give the wall thickness.

### Anecdotal

A designer who ships an MJF-printed mouse and other members of a mouse-modding community described their experience in chat. Nothing in this subsection can be checked by a reader, and none of it is quoted.

| Topic | Report |
|---|---|
| Vendors | The designer tried more than ten MJF vendors and found the variation between them very large. JLC3DP was called cheap but not consistent enough. He still asks for vendor tips, so no bureau is known to be good |
| Warp | Described as solved by stiffening the part with removable rods or a cage and cutting it off after printing. His theory is that most MJF warp comes from the hot dye bath. Another member saw warp depend on where the part sat in the build volume |
| SLS | Shells from a Formlabs Fuse 1+ were the worst prints the designer had seen; SLS is much worse than MJF for thin walls. An HSS sample had too much side roughness |
| Resin | Shells from Formlabs' print service were pretty cheap but too weak for thin walls |
| Finish and weight | Coating adds about 2 g. Dye bleeds when cleaned with alcohol, and home fabric dye works. One side-button shell weighed 9.2 g in MJF and 7.8 g in SLS. Shells have arrived bent from shipping |

The resin report cuts against the 21 g resin shell above. The reading that reconciles them is that the published shell is a thicker-walled design; the case study does not give the wall thickness, so treat this as an inference.

### What follows

MJF PA12 with a stiffening cage, one shell at one bureau, before ordering several. ZELTA3D comes first in Singapore because it runs an HP MJF 5210 in-house with a 4 to 6 working day lead[^zelta-mjf]. JLC3DP is the cheap control, not the first choice. SLA tough resin is for walls well above 1 mm. Your own FDM printer or the free library printer is for fit checks only: the free NLB MakeIT Bambu P1S is PLA only[^nlb], and a Singapore FDM bureau states 0.2 mm depending on geometry on its own page[^zerocore].

## Mouse boards

A mouse board of about 30 x 60 mm with a dense layout and a PixArt sensor combines three limits from the sections above. If it is single-sided, JLC Economic takes it (10 x 10 mm minimum) but a side of 3 cm or less triggers the small-board surcharge. If it is double-sided, JLC Standard needs 70 x 70 mm or more, so the board goes on rails or a panel and pays the US\$51.12 setup and US\$16.42 stencil. QFN parts need X-ray at JLC, which Economic charges US\$1.64 per piece for[^jlc-price]. The sensor is consigned or hand-soldered. For the firmware side of a mouse board, see [Patching an nRF52840 gaming mouse's firmware](/guides/nrf52840-mouse-firmware-patching/).

## Buying from Singapore

### GST at the S\$400 line

Imports by air or post with a CIF value (goods plus insurance plus freight) up to S\$400 enter without GST. Above it, 9 % is charged on the entire value, not on the excess[^iras]. Shipping counts toward the S\$400, as in an IRAS example of S\$390 goods plus S\$35 transport, which is S\$425 and pays GST[^customs-cif][^iras].

A GST-registered overseas vendor must charge GST at checkout on goods up to S\$400 per item, and then gives the courier its registration number so GST is not charged again at import[^iras]. An overseas business must register above S\$1 million of global turnover and S\$100,000 a year of consumer sales into Singapore; below that it is not obliged to[^iras-overseas]. Whether JLC or PCBWay are registered was not found: JLC's customs page says taxes are the customer's and lists thresholds for several countries but not Singapore[^jlc-customs]. Read the GST line on an actual checkout.

DHL's Singapore page, in a passage extracted from a search index, says a minimum of S\$20 or 5 % of the GST payable, whichever is higher and up to S\$100, is charged to recover GST it paid in advance. Whether that applies to a consumer consignee on every dutiable import was not confirmed[^dhl]. FedEx and UPS clearance fees were not found.

### JLC shipping

JLC shipping depends on the carrier. One web cart with a US\$6.80 subtotal and a Singapore address, read 2026-11-21 (measured, one data point and not a tariff):

| Carrier | Cost (US dollars) | Time shown |
|---|---|---|
| DHL Express | 35.42 | 3 to 5 business days |
| FedEx Express | 20.07 | 3 to 5 business days |
| UPS Worldwide Express Saver | 9.52 | 3 to 5 business days |
| Global Standard Direct Line | 7.60 | 14 to 20 business days |
| S.F Express Economy | 13.81 | 5 to 8 business days |

On rev 1, shipping was US\$10.03 by UPS Express Saver through JLCONE, against US\$50.64 on a DHL web cart. Those are two carriers, so the gap is mostly the carrier: UPS cost 9.52 and DHL 35.42 on the cart above. JLC does say that "selected shipping rates and routes" are available only through its JLCONE desktop app[^jlcone], but it does not say by how much. The rev 1 figure may also have carried a new-user shipping coupon, so the comparison is not like for like.

### One cart, or not

| Service | Combines with a JLCPCB checkout? |
|---|---|
| JLC3DP printing | Yes[^jlc3dp-combine] |
| JLCCNC | Yes per JLCCNC's help article (updated 2026-01-15); not tried in a real cart[^jlccnc-combine] |
| JLCMC screws and inserts | No since 2026-11-02; place it as its own order or buy locally[^jlcmc-changes] |
| LCSC components | Own checkout. Joins JLC only if the JLC order ships to LCSC's Hong Kong warehouse first[^lcsc-combine] |

JLCPCB can add an item to an existing order only while it is in production[^jlc-add]. The batch ships once, when the slowest item is ready.

### Coupons

The new-customer package is US\$123 on JLC's coupon page, while two other JLC pages say US\$70 and US\$60, so the account decides[^jlc-coupons]. Monthly, JLC issues US\$25 of SMT coupons. The rules are one coupon per batch, no coupon on a combined or added order, and no use of one order type's coupon on another[^jlc-coupon-use]. Put the shipping coupon on the largest single batch. Black Friday 2026 runs 18 November to 2 December (ET), with up to US\$251 of combined JLCPCB coupon value, US\$47 at JLC3DP and US\$135 at JLCCNC; conditions vary by service and region[^black-friday].
