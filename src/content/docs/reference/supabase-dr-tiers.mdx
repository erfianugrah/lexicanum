A disaster recovery (DR) policy is written in recovery point objective (RPO - how much data you can lose) and recovery time objective (RTO - how
long you are down), and Supabase gives you three tiers with very different
numbers on both. The incident mechanics and the standby build are the
companion docs - [the incident reference](/reference/supabase-incident-resilience/)
and [the resilience runbook](/guides/supabase-resilience-runbook/). This
page is the money-and-recovery-point axis those two deliberately skip.

The measured numbers on this page come from the
edge-resilience lab experiment: logical replication from a managed
primary (ap-southeast-2) to a managed standby (ap-southeast-1): W05
(replication lag) and W06 (dump and restore) on 2026-08-15, W21 (spend cap)
on 2026-08-17, with the Storage numbers from W10 (2026-08-15) and W26
(2026-08-17) and the resize windows from compute-disk D09 (2026-08-19) and
platform-downtime D03 (2026-08-04); the standby build and cutover rehearsal are
in [the runbook](/guides/supabase-resilience-runbook/). The restore figures
come from the restore-paths lab experiment (RP01-RP03, 2026-10-10):
throwaway Pro and Free projects in ap-southeast-1, driven over the
Management API and the shared pooler from one IPv4-only vantage, with n
stated on each figure. The remaining figures are quoted from the cited
Supabase docs and the SLA page, and the evidence table at the end of the
doc splits measured from documented.

**TL;DR** - Daily backups are already on (Pro: 7 days, Team: 14,
Enterprise: 30) with up to a day of RPO.[^sb-backups] PITR is the add-on
that pulls worst-case RPO to ~2 minutes for ~\$100-\$400/month plus a
Small compute floor, and it replaces the daily
backups.[^sb-backups] A PITR restore of a 10 MB database on Small was back
to `ACTIVE_HEALTHY` in 30 s (n=3, measured 2026-10-10); no daily-backup
restore was timed. A hand-rolled warm standby beats both on RPO
(34ms-1s, measured) and RTO (minutes, rehearsed) at the cost of a second
project and owning the cutover. Your own billing state is availability
class zero: under the Fair Use Policy the whole org can answer
402.[^sb-billing-faq]

## The tiers on one axis

| Tier | Worst-case RPO | Expected RTO | Monthly cost | Plan floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daily backups | up to ~24h | size-dependent restore downtime, project offline (documented; not measured) | included | Pro (7d) / Team (14d) / Ent (30d) |
| PITR add-on | ~2 minutes | size-dependent restore downtime; measured 30-81 s to `ACTIVE_HEALTHY` at 10-574 MB on Small, with a narrower outage than "offline" (RP02, 2026-10-10, [below](#pitr-restore-on-small-measured)) | ~\$100 (7d) / ~\$200 (14d) / ~\$400 (28d) + Small compute | Pro + Small compute |
| Warm standby (hand-rolled) | 34ms-1s replication lag (measured) | minutes - a rehearsed cutover, plus a resize window (61-105 s settle across four resizes, up to 17.0 s REST outage on the Small -> Large step, compute-disk D09) if the standby runs smaller than the primary | second project's compute at the size you will cut over to (~\$10 if that is Micro) + your runbook | any paid plan |
| Read replicas | asynchronous; lag not measured | no failover - read-only (Data API `GET`, or SQL over the replica's pooler), no documented promotion | same compute size as the primary, outside the \$10 credits | paid plans |

RPO and RTO are different axes. PITR's "~2 minutes" is a recovery POINT -
how much data you can lose - while its recovery TIME is the size-dependent
restore downtime, which the docs describe as the project being
inaccessible.[^sb-backups] The PITR restore measured here on a small
database showed a narrower outage than that (Tier 2). The
standby flips that: its RPO is the replication lag and its RTO is your
cutover procedure. The two cover different failures - PITR for "we deleted
the wrong rows yesterday", standby for "the region is down now".

The production checklist positions read replicas as the availability
answer to a disk failure event and PITR as the durability answer to the
same event.[^sb-going-prod] Replicas serve reads from another node, but the
read replica docs describe no way to promote one and Auth always goes to
the primary, so they take read load and do not cover an outage.

## Tier 1: Daily backups (already running)

On Pro, Team and Enterprise your project is backed up daily already:
7, 14 and 30 days of retention respectively.[^sb-backups] Free-tier
projects get nothing - the documented self-serve path is a scheduled
`supabase db dump` (or `pg_dump`), kept off-site.[^sb-backups] Size the
schedule off the measured floor: 12.4s to dump and 6.4s to restore 10k
rows through the pooler session host (edge-resilience W06, 2026-08-15);
the command and the host shape are in
[the runbook, Part 5](/guides/supabase-resilience-runbook/).

Restoring from a daily backup has these limits:

- **Storage objects are not in database backups.** The backup holds the
  metadata; restoring an old backup does not bring back objects deleted
  after it.[^sb-backups] Objects do not follow their metadata through
  replication either, so sync them separately and cost the sync. A standby
  answered `400` with `NoSuchBucket` in the body for an object that existed
  on the primary, and the download-then-upload sync of a small object took
  under a second (edge-resilience W10, 2026-08-15). Dual-writing at the
  client lands 200/200 with 107ms skew, but a partial failure (primary 200 /
  standby 400) leaves the object on one side only until a sync-after closes
  it in 97ms (W26, 2026-08-17).
- **Custom role passwords are not stored.** Restore from a daily backup
  and you reset custom-role passwords afterwards.[^sb-backups] That claim
  is documented for daily-backup restore and was not tested. For PITR
  restore the lab measured the `postgres` role through the pooler (RP02,
  2026-10-10, no custom role): 9 of 9 clean restores kept the current
  password and refused the replaced one. The stored verifier was a third
  value each time, matching neither the pre-rotation nor the post-rotation
  fingerprint (first 8 hex characters of `md5(rolpassword)`), so the
  credentials were set again after the restore. That is a fingerprint
  inference, and it matches the 2026-07-30 changelog entry saying current
  credentials are reapplied after every physical restore.[^sb-restore-creds]
  The current password was refused for about one 5 s poll between "database
  answers" and "password works" in 6 of 7 restores observed at that
  resolution, which bounds the stale window at about 10 s without measuring it. Targets here were
  minutes old; a target hours or days old was not run.
- **Restore is offline and size-dependent.** The docs say the project is
  inaccessible during the restore and the downtime grows with the
  database.[^sb-backups] That is documented for daily-backup restore, which
  the lab could not run (the Management API route answered "unavailable",
  below). PITR restore on a small database measured differently: REST, Auth
  and Realtime showed no failed 1 s sample, Storage failed for 12 s, and the
  pooler for 5-13 s (41 s at 574 MB). Storage's 12 s was 8 of 9 restores.
  The two cases differ, and neither is generalised beyond the sizes tested.
  Mid-incident, restoring to a NEW project keeps the original up - the
  dashboard and the clone-project flow both offer it.[^sb-backups] The
  Management API documents no clone path: the published OpenAPI document (115
  paths) has none, the create-project body has no source field, and the
  Dashboard's clone route answers a PAT `401` `Unsupported access token`.
  The entitlements `project_cloning` and `backup.restore_to_new_project`
  read true on the Pro org. Clone was not run, so the password and Storage
  behaviour after a clone are unread (RP01, 2026-10-10).[^sb-clone]

## Tier 2: PITR (the add-on)

Point-in-Time Recovery archives WAL with WAL-G on top of daily physical
snapshots: WAL files ship every two minutes by default (immediately past
a size threshold, never when idle), so the worst-case recovery point is
two minutes back.[^sb-backups]

- It costs ~\$100, ~\$200 or ~\$400 per month for 7, 14 or 28 days of
  recovery retention, billed hourly, and the spend cap explicitly does NOT
  cover it, because it is an opted-in add-on.[^sb-backups][^sb-cost-control]
- The project must run at least Small compute.[^sb-backups]
- **Enabling PITR stops the daily backups** - finer granularity makes
  both unnecessary. Disabling PITR later leaves physical backups only,
  which restore but cannot be downloaded.[^sb-backups]
- **Replication topologies must be unwound first.** If the project uses
  subscriptions or replication slots, drop them before the restore and
  recreate them after; only the Realtime slot is exempted and handled
  automatically.[^sb-backups] A project acting as your standby's
  publication source loses its slots in a restore, and the standby's
  subscription dies with it and must be recreated with a re-sync.
  Inventory them first with `select * from pg_replication_slots`;
  `max_replication_slots` is 10 on Micro and on Small (compute-disk D01).
  A dropped subscription left its slot on the publisher both after a plain
  drop (edge-resilience W05) and after `slot_name = none` then drop (W14),
  so list `pg_replication_slots` on the publisher and
  `pg_drop_replication_slot` any orphan first. A forgotten slot retains WAL
  and grows the disk.

### PITR restore on Small, measured

The lab ran `POST database/backups/restore-pitr` on 2026-10-10 against a Pro
project on Small compute with `pitr_7` applied by the test and removed
afterwards, a 2 GB gp3 disk, a seed row, a bucket and an object (RP02, five
runs, ap-southeast-1). Each run restored to an instant before a password
rotation, then rotated again and restored to an instant after it as a
control: 9 clean restores, 4 with the pre-rotation target and 5 controls.
Every target was minutes old.

| Measurement | Value | n |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status back to `ACTIVE_HEALTHY` (10 s polling), 10 MB database | 30 s | 3 |
| Same, control targets after a rotation | 30, 30, 40, 40, 41 s | 5 |
| Same, 574 MB database, pre-rotation target | 81 s | 1 |
| REST, Auth and Realtime (1 s probes) | no failed sample in any restore (one simultaneous timeout on all three 338 s after one run's first request, no recovery recorded, not explained) | 9 |
| Storage path | failed for 12 s in 8 restores, 24 s in the other; error text `The operation timed out`, never an HTTP 5xx | 9 |
| Pooler login (5 s poll) | 5-13 s on 10 MB and on a 715 MB control; 41 s at 574 MB pre-rotation; 23 s on the 574 MB control | 9 |
| First `restore-pitr` attempt | refused `400` `Recovery time target must be within range` with the range named in 8 of 9; accepted on a retry 60 s or 121 s later | 9 |
| Target against the quoted upper bound | 19-96 s past it where recorded | 4 records |
| Window right after enabling `pitr_7` | the first read already had `pitr_enabled=true` and a window of 359 or 360 s | 5 |

Reading the table:

- The 574 MB control restored in 40 s and the 715 MB control in 41 s, so
  the 81 s at 574 MB is a single point, too few for a size curve. Retries ran every
  60 s, so the acceptance lag is bounded by that step and not resolved. The
  reason the window at enable time ends before the create call is not known.
- The pooler error texts during the restores were
  `the database system is not accepting connections`, `is starting up`,
  `is shutting down`, `econnrefused` and `terminating connection due to
  administrator command`.
- After `pitr_7` was applied, `GET database/backups` listed zero entries and
  carried the window in `physical_backup_data`.

Other behaviour seen across the restores and a Free-plan unpause (RP02 and
RP03, 2026-10-10):

- Storage did not return a `500` after any restore or unpause: list,
  download (bytes equal) and upload answered `200`. One PITR restore to
  before an object's upload made its download `404` `not_found` and the
  listing omit it, and the same path was writable again without upsert (n=1).
  What happened to the file in object storage was not read.
- `GET /health` read `realtime=COMING_UP` in the read taken right after the
  restore or unpause in 4 of 8 runs.
- A password change while a Free project was paused was refused: `400`
  `Cannot reset password for non-active projects` (3 of 3 runs, RP03). The
  stale-credential condition the changelog describes[^sb-restore-creds]
  cannot be set up through that route; another route was not tried.
- The pooler's circuit breaker is a hazard for restore probes. Polling a
  wrong password every 4 s opened it within about a minute, and
  `(ECIRCUITBREAKER) too many authentication failures, new connections are
  temporarily blocked` then answered every login, including the correct
  password, before the password was checked. Poll a known-wrong password at
  most once a minute, and read `blocked` as unanswered.
- Small's disk is 2 GB gp3 and WAL counts against it. Growing a PITR project
  to 574 MB left 505 MB free of 1981 MB with 736 MB of WAL. Asking for 1500
  MB filled the disk at 715 MB; the next password rotation failed and the
  restore request was refused `400` `Database appears to be unreachable`
  until 301 s. What filled the disk was not separated (WAL not yet
  archived, or something else), and the WAL figure is from the 574 MB run
  only. [The compute and disk reference](/reference/supabase-compute-disk/)
  has the other `pg_wal` measurements on a 2 GB disk.

What a Pro project can reach through the Management API (RP01, n=1, one fresh
project, entitlement `project_pausing` false):

| Call | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /pause` | `400` `Project is not free-tier` |
| `POST database/backups/restore` with the listed physical backup | `400` `This endpoint is unavailable at the moment` |
| `GET database/backups/restore-point` | `400` `This endpoint is unavailable at the moment` |
| `POST database/backups/restore-pitr` without the add-on | `400` `PITR is not enabled for this project.` |

The two `unavailable` answers also came from a manual call (no artifact) a few
minutes after `pitr_7` was applied. Whether they differ on an older project or
an org with another flag was not tested, so read them as "not run". The pause refusal matches the legacy-project result in
[the platform operation cost reference](/reference/supabase-platform-operation-cost/).

## Tier 3: the hand-rolled warm standby

This is the tier the lab measured: logical replication managed-to-managed,
34ms-1057ms lag cross-region, cutover rehearsed in minutes, sessions
portable via third-party-auth registration. The build and every caveat are
in [the runbook](/guides/supabase-resilience-runbook/); this page covers
the cost:

- **A second project at the compute you will cut over to.** Micro is
  ~\$10/month; the \$10 monthly compute credits cover ONE project, so the
  second one is real money.[^sb-compute] Pricing the standby at Micro and
  resizing at cutover moves the resize window into your RTO: 61-105 s to
  settle across four resizes, the Small -> Large step settling in 61 s
  with 17.0 s of contiguous REST outage (compute-disk D09, 2026-08-19),
  or 131 s on Auth and 207 s on the
  pooler for Micro -> Small on another rig (platform-downtime D03,
  2026-08-04). The full sizing table plus resize windows and quota
  behaviour are in the [compute and disk reference](/reference/supabase-compute-disk/).
- **A read replica does not substitute for the standby.** A replica runs at
  the primary's compute size, its compute is not covered by the
  credits,[^sb-read-replicas] and the read replica docs describe no way to
  promote a replica or fail over to one (doc-read 2026-10-08; see
  [read replicas](/reference/supabase-read-replicas/)). It is a
  read-scaling feature that happens to survive a disk failure.
- **Rehearsal is a monthly cost.** The standby only beats PITR on RTO if
  the cutover is rehearsed - sequence resync, config re-apply, routing
  flip - and rehearsal is your time every month.

<Aside type="caution" title="Do not stack tiers by accident">
Enabling PITR silently stops the daily backups, and a restore on a
project with subscriptions unwinds your standby topology. Both are easy
to discover mid-incident. Pick the tier per failure mode - PITR for data
loss, standby for availability - and write down which one is armed.
</Aside>

## Class zero: your own billing state

Some org-wide outages are not the platform at all.
Under the Fair Use Policy, service restrictions apply when an
organization continually exceeds the Free quota, exceeds the Pro quota
with the spend cap on, or has overdue invoices:[^sb-billing-faq]

- projects paused, databases switched to read-only, new launches blocked
- every API request answered **402** with a restriction description in
  the body
- applied to ALL projects in the org; dashboard data access is retained

There is a notification and grace period first (except suspected abuse).
Removal: fix the cause (pay, update the card, disable the cap, or reduce
usage); usage-limit restrictions also lift when the quota refills at the
next billing cycle, and immediately on upgrading or disabling the spend
cap.[^sb-billing-faq] In a probe table, map 402 to billing rather than to a
platform fault. The spend cap setting is the trade-off: cap on means
eventual restriction, cap off means an uncapped bill.[^sb-cost-control]

The spend cap does not act as a request-path circuit breaker. Tripping the Pro transform quota by 5 past
100 returned 200 on every render - no synchronous disallow at the
boundary (measured, edge-resilience W21, 2026-08-17). The documented
"further usage disallowed" rides the billing path - notification, grace
period, then the restrictions above - so the first signal you can
detect is still the notification, not an API error at quota+1.

## What the SLA does and does not cover

**The uptime SLA is Enterprise-only.** 99.9% per product per calendar
month, measured per-project, per-region or globally depending on the
product, and only for generally-available features - Beta and Alpha are
outside it.[^sb-sla][^sb-features] The remedy is service credits: 10%,
15%, 20% or 30% of the affected service's monthly fees by availability
band, capped at 20% of the trailing twelve months' fees, and the credits
are the sole remedy. Below Enterprise there is no uptime SLA at all -
which is most of why the hand-rolled tier exists.

**Support SLAs exist for Team and Enterprise.** Urgent response is 24
hours 24/7x365 on Team and 1 hour 24/7x365 on Enterprise Standard and
Priority Plus; business-hours limits start at High (Team: 1 business day
for High and Normal, 2 business days for Low).[^sb-sla] Pro gets support
access with no SLA. And on Team or Enterprise, the documented pre-launch
channel is a support ticket with at least 2 weeks' notice before a
launch or heavy load event.

When you build availability on top of a non-GA feature, you are outside
the contract by construction, which is another reason to build the
hand-rolled tier from GA primitives (logical replication, the Management
API) rather than platform HA features.

## Which do I pick

| Your requirement | Tier | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Oops, we dropped the wrong table this morning" | PITR | restore target chosen to the second, just before the mistake; the ~2-minute worst-case RPO bounds only WAL not yet shipped, and WAL from this morning has shipped |
| Compliance says "we have backups" | Daily backups | already on; just prove the restore |
| Free tier, any durability at all | scheduled `db dump` off-site | the only option that exists |
| Region down, RTO in minutes | Warm standby | the only tier that is up somewhere else |
| Read load, plus disk-failure cover | Read replicas | availability for reads; never a failover |
| Zero budget, best possible | cron `pg_dump` + object sync | measured floor: 12.4s dump / 6.4s restore at 10k rows |
