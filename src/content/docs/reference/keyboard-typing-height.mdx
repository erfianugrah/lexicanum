How high a keyboard's keytops sit above the desk, what the ergonomics standards and studies say about that height, and how the case, the switch and the keycap profile add up to it. The numbers are worked through on auspex, an open 5x12 ortholinear Hall-effect keyboard (the [series hub](/reference/auspex-hall-effect-keyboard/) covers the whole board), but the limits and the stack arithmetic apply to any board with MX-style switches.

Three kinds of number appear below, and each is labelled where it appears. Standard or study: from a published standard or paper, footnoted. Measured: by the author on 2026-11-05, either with calipers (three MT3 keycap rows) or from the desk to the keytop of the author's own keyboard. Modelled: stack arithmetic, the wrist-geometry model, and keycap heights taken from manufacturer drawings or community CAD. Absolute modelled heights carry about +-1 mm from three inputs nobody has measured yet (the switch's keycap seat, the pad thickness, the MT3 R4 cap); differences between rows, profiles and case variants are exact, because the same assumptions cancel.

**TL;DR:**

- The standards agree on one number: home row at most 30 mm above the desk (preferred, "as low as possible"), 35 mm absolute cap, slope 0-12 deg recommended and 0-15 deg allowed. ISO 9241-410, ANSI/HFES 100-2007, the HFES 100-2026 draft and DGUV 215-410 say the same thing.
- Lower helps, but gently. In the geometry model each millimetre of keytop height costs about 0.34 deg of wrist extension, so 30.5 -> 25 mm saves about 2 deg; finger curl alone moves the answer by +-6 deg. Carpal tunnel pressure climbs steeply only past about 25-30 deg of extension. A palm rest level with the keytops cuts modelled extension to about 11 deg, more than any change in keyboard height.
- On an MX-style board everything above the PCB is fixed by the switch: plate top 5.0 mm above the PCB top, keycap rim 6.2 mm above the plate. Only the pads, the floor, the standoff and the keycap profile are free.
- With a bottom-mounted USB-C receptacle the lowest PCB position is set by the cable plug overmold (7.0-7.4 mm tall, centred on the receptacle), once the case's plug window is cut through the floor: the PCB bottom has to sit about 5.8 mm above the desk.
- auspex, flat: home row 26.01 mm with Cherry caps, 26.85 mm with DSA, 30.21 mm with MT3. The previous case put MT3 at 34.05 mm. The author's current keyboard measures 30.5 mm at the second row from the bottom.
- No study found keycap profile affecting typing speed, keycap wobble or rapid-trigger behaviour. Choose a profile on height and on how its rows map to the layout: Cherry is the lowest sculpted profile, DSA is uniform so any cap fits any key, MT3 sits on the 30 mm line.

## What the standards say

Four documents, one number. ISO 9241-410 Annex B.2.3.1 b: "The preferred home row height is as low as possible and not greater than 30 mm. However, the home row height of the keyboard shall not exceed 35 mm."[^iso9241-410] B.2.3.2, for compact keyboards (which a 60-key 5x12 is closer to), repeats the 30 and 35 mm figures. ANSI/HFES 100-2007 section 6.2.2.3 gives the same pair and defines the measuring point: "Keyboard height is the height measured from the table surface to the middle of the home row of the keyboard."[^hfes100-2007] The HFES 100-2026 public review draft keeps both figures and the measuring point, and makes the unadjusted 0-15 deg slope range a "shall".[^hfes100-2026] The German workplace guidance DGUV Information 215-410 asks for at most 30 mm in the middle key row and 0-12 deg of slope with the feet folded in, 15 deg with them out.[^dguv-215-410]

| Item | ISO 9241-410 | ANSI/HFES 100-2007 | HFES 100-2026 draft | DGUV 215-410 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home-row height | preferred as low as possible, at most 30 mm; shall not exceed 35 mm | preferred 30 mm or less, should not exceed 35 mm | same as 2007 | at most 30 mm (should) |
| Slope, unadjusted | recommended 0-12 deg; shall be 0-15 deg positive | at least one setting in 0-15 deg | shall be 0-15 deg | 0-12 deg; at most 15 deg with feet out |
| Negative slope | not mentioned | allowed for alternative designs | allowed for alternative designs | not mentioned |
| Key pitch | 19 +- 1 mm | not checked | not checked | - |
| Palm rest | if present, 50-100 mm deep in front of the bottom row | supports should be provided for prolonged use; under 3.8 cm deep may raise contact pressure | optional; match or exceed the keyboard's front-edge height | 100-150 mm of free desk in front; an extra rest can hinder |
| Wrist posture limit | - | extension and flexion each under 30 deg | - | - |

Measure the home row at the centre of its keytop, with the feet on, as HFES defines it. The ISO text read here does not define the point. Positive slope means the bottom row is lower than the top row. A 19.05 mm ortholinear grid meets the ISO 19 +- 1 mm pitch.

One commercial reference point: Wooting's design guideline for its 80HE is a front height with keycaps of at most 30 mm; the board measures 19.4 mm at the front without caps and 28.4 mm with its OEM caps, and Wooting writes that a 0 deg prototype under 30 mm "was not possible" with its daughterboard and gasket stack.[^wooting-80he]

## What the studies show

### Carpal tunnel pressure against wrist extension

Rempel, Keir and Bach measured carpal tunnel pressure (CTP) in 20 subjects while they typed at set wrist postures.[^rempel-2008] Their results table:

| Target extension | Measured extension | CTP while typing |
|---|---|---|
| ~0 deg | - | 1.9 kPa (14 mmHg) |
| 15 deg | 16.1 deg | 2.3 kPa (17 mmHg) |
| 30 deg | 27.9 deg | 3.1 kPa (23 mmHg) |
| 45 deg | 38.1 deg | 4.0 kPa (30 mmHg) |
| their subjects on a conventional keyboard | 24.8 deg | 2.8 kPa (21 mmHg) |

Typing raised CTP over the same static posture (p = 0.001). Keir, Bach, Hudes and Rempel (n = 37) worked out the 25th-percentile angles, the postures that keep 75% of people under a pressure threshold: 26.6 deg of extension for 25 mmHg and 32.7 deg (95% CI 27.2-38.1) for 30 mmHg. Ulnar deviation has the tightest limits, 12.1 and 14.5 deg.[^keir-2007] The lowest CTP in controls sat at 2 +- 9 deg of extension, not exactly 0,[^weiss-1995] and people asked to find a rest posture blindfolded chose 7-9 deg of extension.[^fagarasanu-2004] So a few degrees of extension are not harmful by this data; the steep part of the curve starts past about 25-30 deg.

Pressure on the palm matters for rest design: in cadavers, 1 kg on the flexor retinaculum raised CTP to 103 mmHg.[^cobb-1995] A palm rest should carry the heel of the palm, not the carpal tunnel region.

### Slope

Every posture study read here finds less wrist extension at 0 deg or negative slope than at positive slope.

- Simoneau and Marklin (n = 30) varied slope from +15 to -15 deg. Mean extension fell from 22 to 9 deg, about 0.43 deg of extension per degree of slope (derived, 13 / 30). Height relative to the elbow mattered more: 21.8 deg with the keyboard below elbow height against 7.3 deg above it.[^simoneau-2001] That height is the desk and chair setup, not the keyboard's own thickness.
- Simoneau, Marklin and Berman (n = 16): about 12 deg of extension at +7.5 deg slope became 3 deg of flexion at -15 deg; forearm muscle activity changed by only 1-3% MVC.[^simoneau-2003]
- 90 professional typists on a flat keyboard with a +5 deg slope typed at 21.2 +- 8.8 deg (left) and 17.0 +- 7.4 deg (right) of extension.[^simoneau-1999]
- Hedge and Powers: a self-chosen negative-slope tray averaged 12 deg below horizontal and took extension from 13 deg to -1 deg.[^hedge-1995] A 3-week field trial of a tilt-down tray raised the share of neutral-zone wrist movements from about 50% to over 80%.[^hedge-1999] Both used trays below desk height, which a desktop case cannot copy: on a desk, a raised front edge only raises the bottom rows.
- Eghujovbo and Kim (n = 30): users prefer positive angles, but negative tilt reduced muscle activity and awkward wrist posture "while maintaining performance".[^eghujovbo-2024]

The standards want 0-12 deg positive and allow negative slope only for alternative designs; the posture studies favour 0 deg or below; users prefer positive. Flat satisfies both the standards and the studies.

### Height in the field

A prospective study of 632 new computer users found that "use of a keyboard with the 'J' key > 3.5 cm above the table surface ... was associated with a greater risk" of hand and arm symptoms or disorders, and that a J key more than 12 cm from the desk edge, which leaves room for the forearms on the desk, was associated with lower risk.[^ajim-2002] The study's NIOSH final report defines J-key height as table surface to J keytop.[^niosh-2002] The 3.5 cm threshold coincides with the 35 mm cap, but it is a risk association: the keyboards of that period differed in more than height, and no mechanism was isolated.

The cross-sectional study before it (n = 379) found wrist extension "weakly correlated with keyboard height (r = -0.24) and virtually not at all with keyboard thickness (r = 0.07)", and wrist rest use associated with less flexion (21.9 against 25.1 deg).[^gerr-2000] Serina, Tal and Rempel (n = 25) found that wrist angles "were not predictable based on anthropometry or typing speed and varied widely between subjects".[^serina-1999] Any single best height is a population statement.

### Floating against supported hands

- Forearm support gave less ulnar deviation and discomfort than floating; wrist support lowered shoulder muscle activity (n = 13).[^cook-2004]
- A 1-year randomised trial with 182 call-centre operators: a wide forearm support halved neck and shoulder disorders (hazard ratio 0.49, 95% CI 0.24-0.97).[^rempel-2006]
- Hedge and Powers found that full-motion forearm supports did not change wrist posture.[^hedge-1995]

DGUV takes the other side: a low, flat keyboard makes a palm rest unnecessary, and a rest can get in the way.[^dguv-215-410] ANSI/HFES 100-2007 says supports should be provided for prolonged use.[^hfes100-2007]

### Gaming

No study varies keyboard height or slope for gaming. One small motion-capture study (n = 5, first-person shooter, 5 minutes per device) found the keyboard among the devices with the highest left-wrist ulnar deviation.[^mariano-2023] The model below adds an inference: a hand curled over WASD flexes its fingers more than a touch-typing hand, and at the same keytop height that needs about 5 deg more extension. A low board helps the gaming hand most.

### Speed and keycap profile

No source found a typing-speed penalty from flat or negative slope. HFES 100-2007 6.2.2.4: "Research examining stepped and sloped keyboard profiles found no notable differences in user performance (Kinkead & Gonzales, 1969)."[^hfes100-2007] No study was found that measures keycap wobble per profile, rapid-trigger false actuations against cap height or mass, or typing speed across modern keycap profiles.

One piece of reasoning, not measurement: hobbyist scales put common caps at 0.80-1.35 g,[^gh-keycap-stats] and a 2 g cap on a spring with 30 gf of initial force is under 7% of the preload, so cap weight alone cannot move a switch at rest. Whether a taller, heavier cap makes rapid-trigger false releases more or less likely depends on stem play and the firmware's sensitivity, and is a bench question.

## The wrist-geometry model

To put the standards' millimetres into degrees, a sagittal-plane model: a rigid hand pivots about the wrist joint centre, and the fingertip has to land on the home-row keytop. It is a sensitivity model, not a measurement, and every input is an assumption:

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Forearm | horizontal (the ISO/HFES reference posture); extension = hand axis angle above the forearm line |
| Palm length (wrist centre to knuckle) | 116 mm male, 108 mm female: ANSUR II medians[^ansur-ii] |
| Finger length | hand length minus palm length: 193 - 116 = 77 mm male, 180 - 108 = 72 mm female |
| Finger curl | MCP 20, PIP 45, DIP 20 deg flexion; phalanges split 0.45 / 0.30 / 0.25 of finger length (assumed) |
| Wrist joint centre | 20 mm above whatever the wrist or palm heel rests on (about half a wrist depth) |
| Result of the curl | fingertip 160 mm ahead of and 52 mm below the wrist centre (male median; female 149 / 49 mm) |

Wrist resting on the desk, male median (female within 0.5 deg):

| Home-row keytop above desk | 15 mm | 20 mm | 25 mm | 30.5 mm | 35 mm | 40 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wrist extension | 16.3 deg | 18.0 deg | 19.7 deg | 21.6 deg | 23.1 deg | 24.8 deg |

- The slope near 30 mm is 0.341 deg per mm. 30.5 -> 25 mm saves 1.9 deg; 30.5 -> 20 mm saves 3.6 deg.
- At a 30.5 mm keytop, finger curl changes the answer more than height does: 10/30/10 deg curl gives 14.5 deg of extension, 30/60/30 deg gives 26.8 deg. Moving the wrist centre to 15 or 25 mm gives 23.3 or 19.9 deg. That fits the weak correlations in Gerr 2000 and the spread in Serina 1999.
- The model's 21.6 deg at 30.5 mm falls inside the 17-25 deg measured on conventional keyboards by Simoneau 1999, Serina 1999 and Rempel 2008. That is a sanity check, not a validation.
- A tighter gaming curl (30/60/30) gives 22.7 / 24.7 / 26.8 deg at 20 / 25 / 30.5 mm, about 5 deg over the typing curl.

With a palm rest, the wrist centre sits 20 mm above the rest top:

| Keytop, rest top | 0 mm | 10 mm | 18 mm | 25 mm | 30.5 mm | 35 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 mm keytop | 18.0 deg | 14.6 deg | 11.8 deg | 9.4 deg | 7.5 deg | 6.0 deg |
| 25 mm keytop | 19.7 deg | 16.3 deg | 13.6 deg | 11.2 deg | 9.3 deg | 7.7 deg |
| 30.5 mm keytop | 21.6 deg | 18.2 deg | 15.4 deg | 13.0 deg | 11.2 deg | 9.6 deg |

A rest whose top is level with the keytop gives about 11 deg at either height, close to the 7-9 deg people choose blindfolded and far under the 26.6 deg CTP threshold. Zero extension with this curl needs the wrist centre about 52 mm above the keytop, 82.5 mm above the desk at a 30.5 mm keytop: that is the floating posture, or a keyboard below elbow level.

Tilt costs height. With the wedge's 1.5 mm front and the home row 52.1 mm behind the case front, a tilt raises the home row by 4.2 / 6.0 / 7.8 / 10.5 / 12.3 mm at 3 / 5 / 7 / 10 / 12 deg. By the height model, 5 deg adds 2.0 deg of extension; by the measured slope coefficients (0.43-0.67 deg per degree of slope, Simoneau 2001 and 2003) it adds 2.2-3.4 deg. The two overlap in mechanism, because the studies' slope changes also moved the rows up and down, so do not add them.

## The stack, desk to keytop

For a flat tray-mounted case:

```
keytop = pad proud of case bottom + floor + standoff + PCB + 5.0 + 6.2 + keycap height
```

The two fixed terms come from the switch. The Cherry MX datasheet puts the plate seat 5.0 mm above the housing bottom and the stem top 3.6 mm above an 11.6 mm housing, so 10.2 mm above the plate top.[^cherry-mx] Gateron's KS-20T magnetic switch drawing gives the same 5.00 +- 0.05 mm plate seat, so the plate top sits 5.0 mm above the PCB top whatever the plate thickness.[^gateron-ks20] The keycap rim sits 6.2 mm above the plate top at rest, the MX value in the Cosmos keyboard generator;[^cosmos-switches] that leaves 4.0 mm of the 10.2 mm stem inside the keycap socket. The Gateron drawing does not dimension the stem, so for a magnetic switch the 6.2 mm is Cherry-derived and carries +-0.5 mm.

| Floor | 2.5 | 2.0 | 0.51x the bending stiffness, still 0.8 mm under the foot wells |
| Standoff, floor top to PCB bottom | 6.75 (SWD header 4.25 + 2.5 clearance) | 4.26 (USB-C receptacle 3.26 + 1.0 clearance) | SWD header moved over its own floor opening; clearance cut |
| PCB | 1.6 | 1.6 | - |
| PCB top -> plate top | 5.0 | 5.0 | fixed by the switch |
| Plate top -> keycap rim | 6.2 | 6.2 | fixed by the switch |
| Desk -> keycap rim | 23.35 | 19.46 | 3.89 mm lower at every key |
| MT3 home row (cap R3) | 34.05 | 30.21 | R3 10.7 mm (clone) before, 10.75 mm (caliper) now |

Only the first three rows are free. Plate thickness does not move the keycaps.

With a wedge under the case, the case rotates about its front-bottom edge and the pads move to the wedge's underside:

```
keytop = pad + wedge front + y sin(a) + z cos(a)
```

where `y` is the row centre's distance from the case front and `z` the keytop's height above the case bottom. On auspex the home row is 52.13 mm behind the front face of the 3D-printed case (3.0 mm wall, 0.5 mm gap, 1.0 mm PCB margin, 2.5 key pitches).

## Keycap heights per row

Keycap height here is rim to the highest point of the top surface, mm. On a 5x12 the rows from the back are number, top alpha, home, bottom alpha and modifiers; sculpted profiles call them R1 to R4 with the modifier row taking R4, except KAT, which numbers from the bottom (R1 bottom row, R5 number row).[^susuwatari-ortho][^kat-blog]

| Profile | Number row | Top alpha | Home | Bottom alpha | Modifiers | Source and kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MT3 | 13.89 (R1) | 11.08 (R2) | 10.75 (R3) | 11.6 (R4) | 11.6 (R4) | R1-R3 measured: calipers on the author's MT3 Susuwatari set; R4 modelled, from the KeyV2 clone[^keyv2] |
| Cherry | 9.8 | 7.45 | 6.55 | 7.35 | 7.35 | modelled: KeyV2 clone; no primary Cherry drawing found |
| DSA | 7.39 | 7.39 | 7.39 | 7.39 | 7.39 | manufacturer drawing, 0.291 in[^sp-dsa] |
| KAT | 13.54 (R5) | 11.65 (R4) | 10.67 (R3) | 9.06 (R2) | 10.68 (R1) | bounding boxes of Keyreative's official STEP model[^kat-blog] |
| SA | 14.89 | 12.925 | 11.73 | 12.925 | 12.925 | home row from the manufacturer drawing (0.462 in);[^sp-sa] other rows modelled, KeyV2 |
| XDA | 9.1 | 9.1 | 9.1 | 9.1 | 9.1 | modelled: riskable keycap_playground clone[^riskable] |

KeyV2 says of its own MT3 file: "This is an imperfect attempt to clone the MT3 profile". The caliper values bear that out: KeyV2 gives 13.1 / 10.7 / 10.7 mm for R1-R3, against the measured 13.89 / 11.08 / 10.75. Where KeyV2 and the Cosmos table disagree on Cherry (Cosmos gives 9.3 / 7.0 / 6.0 / 7.0), the taller KeyV2 set is used.[^cosmos-keycaps]

The MT3 designer sets the home-row top at -7 deg so that it lies flat on a keyboard tilted 5-7 deg, and designed the profile for a 5-9 deg board.[^matt3o-mt3] On a flat case MT3's home row faces the typist slightly.

Uniform profiles (DSA, XDA) have no row assignment: any cap goes on any key, which suits an ortholinear board whose layers move keys around. Sculpted sets have to ship an ortho kit; the MT3 Susuwatari kit does.[^susuwatari-ortho]

## What stops a board going lower

The receptacle of auspex's USB-C port is on the bottom of the PCB. A cable plug is centred on the receptacle, 1.63 mm below the PCB bottom (half of the 3.26 mm receptacle), and its overmold is 7.0 mm tall at the USB-IF maximum, up to 7.4 mm on real cables. So the plug reaches 5.13-5.33 mm below the PCB bottom. While the case's plug window starts at the floor top, the standoff has to be at least 6.13 mm. Cut the window down through the floor and the desk becomes the limit: the PCB bottom has to sit about 5.8 mm above the desk (5.33 + 0.5 mm of air).

The current case is about 0.9 mm above that floor (PCB bottom 0.4 + 2.0 + 4.26 = 6.66 mm above the desk, computed); a 7.4 mm overmold clears the desk by 1.33 mm. Pushing the rest of the way, with a floor pocket under the receptacle, would put MT3 at about the author's current keyboard height. Below that, only a PCB change (moving the USB-C receptacle) or a right-angle cable helps.

Each lever, and what it saved at every key:

| Lever | Saving | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| SWD header (4.25 mm, the tallest bottom part) over its own floor opening, exempt from the standoff | 0.99 mm | the debug plug enters from below anyway; the header must stay inside the opening |
| Component clearance under the PCB 2.5 -> 1.0 mm | 1.5 mm | the 2.5 mm was the SWD plug's room; needs the plug window through the floor |
| Plug window cut through the floor | enables the two above | cable plug clears the desk |
| Floor 2.5 -> 2.0 mm | 0.5 mm | 0.51x bending stiffness |
| Pads 2.5 mm (assumed) -> 1.6 mm in the same 1.2 mm wells | 0.9 mm | 0.4 mm proud, still clear of the desk |
| Case rim plate top + 2.0 -> + 1.0 mm | 1.0 mm at the case edge, 0 at the keys | at 3.2 mm of travel the keycap skirt is still 2.0 mm above the rim |
| CNC floor 10.0 -> 2.0 mm, brass moved into the walls | 8.0 mm (CNC) | mass from the walls instead of the floor |

## The reference board

The author types on a Boardsource Equals 60 (a 5x12) with MT3 Susuwatari caps. Measured from the desk (bottom of the rubber bumpers) to the top of the second row from the bottom, it stands about 30.5 mm, and feels a bit high: a reference, not a target. On a 5x12 that row is the bottom alpha row, an MT3 R4 cap, not the home row the standards measure.

Its case is 14 mm tall[^equals-60] and flat, with no incline.[^equals-flat] Working back from the 30.5 mm (inferred, no teardown): 30.5 - 11.6 (R4 cap) - 6.2 (keycap seat) = 12.7 mm from desk to plate top, which leaves about 4-4.5 mm of floor and standoff under the PCB, against 9.25 mm in auspex's previous case. Its home row would sit at about 29.6 mm (30.5 minus the MT3 R4-R3 difference). The current auspex case with the same MT3 caps sits 0.56 mm above it at that row (31.06 mm); with Cherry or DSA caps it sits 3.7 mm below.

The author's keymap for the same board is the subject of the [QMK and VIA keymap guide](/guides/qmk-via-keymap-guide/).

```dot
  sph     [label="KAT or MT3\nhome 30.1-30.2 mm, tilt up to 2 deg"];
  cherry  [label="Cherry\nhome 26.01 mm, tilt up to 7 deg"];
  rest    [label="add a palm rest level\nwith the keytops"];

  start -> remap;
  remap -> dsa    [label="yes"];
  remap -> dish   [label="no"];
  dish  -> sph    [label="yes"];
  dish  -> cherry [label="no"];
  dsa    -> rest  [label="optional"];
  sph    -> rest  [label="optional"];
  cherry -> rest  [label="optional"];
}
```

The same decision as a list (auspex case, heights modelled):

1. If layers or game remaps move caps between rows, use DSA: uniform, home row 26.85 mm, tilt up to 6 deg under the 35 mm cap.
2. Otherwise, if you want MT3's spherical dish, use KAT (home 30.13 mm, its bottom alpha row 2.5 mm lower than MT3's) or MT3 (30.21 mm). Both sit on the 30 mm preferred limit and allow at most 2 deg of tilt.
3. Otherwise use Cherry: the lowest sculpted profile, home row 26.01 mm, tilt up to 7 deg.
4. For any of them, a palm rest gives more than any height change: top about level with the keytops (about 25 mm here), at least 38 mm deep (HFES) or 50-100 mm (ISO), under the heel of the palm. Leave forearm room on the desk: a J key more than 12 cm from the desk edge was protective, which on auspex puts the case front about 7 cm back.

Keep the default flat. A tilt adds height at every row behind the pivot and adds extension; the standards allow it, the studies do not reward it.
