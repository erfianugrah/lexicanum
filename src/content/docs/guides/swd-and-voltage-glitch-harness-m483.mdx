Bringing up SWD and a MOSFET crowbar glitcher on a live consumer peripheral has a narrower safety margin than bench work on a bare development board. While probing my own the vendor the 8 kHz mouse mouse (covered from the USB host side in [8 kHz mouse polling on Windows](/guides/8khz-mouse-merged-reports-windows/)) to study its locked Nuvoton `M483SIDAE` microcontroller, three hardware traps surfaced before a single valid fault trial could run: stock OpenOCD adapter initialisation asserted resets and enabled target power, failed SWD transactions echoed ADC voltage words back as fake chip IDs, and connecting a powered target rail to a Faultier crowbar line back-fed the glitcher's RP2040 through its GPIO ESD diodes until Windows refused to start the USB device with error code 10.

This guide covers the physical bring-up, rail-ownership rules, patched safe-connect OpenOCD driver, and logic-analyzer pulse verification for a read-only SWD and crowbar harness. The companion reference, [Nuvoton M483 flash locks and the glitch-oracle trap](/reference/m483-flash-locks-and-glitch-oracles/), covers the Flash Memory Controller (`FMC`) lock architecture and why a fault-injection sweep on this target fails without an address-sensitive readback oracle.

## Step 1: Unpowered continuity and short isolation

Adjacent pins on a `0.5 mm`-pitch `LQFP-64` package bridge easily under angled microprobe needles, and semiconductor junctions inside the MCU can trigger a multimeter continuity buzzer even when no metallic short exists.

1. Disconnect every USB cable (target mouse, Nu-Link Pro, Faultier, and logic analyzer) before switching the multimeter to resistance or continuity mode. Never probe a powered board in resistance or continuity mode: touching pin 51 (`VDD`) to ground with a continuity source injects current into the rail and causes an immediate USB reset.
2. Measure end-to-end resistance along each intended wire path from the tool header to the target pad:
   - Nu-Link pin 2 (`VCC`) to pin 51 (`VDD` / `H4`): under `2 ohm`.
   - Nu-Link pin 4 (`ICE_DAT`) to pin 33 (`SWDIO` / `H3`): under `2 ohm`.
   - Nu-Link pin 6 (`ICE_CLK`) to pin 34 (`SWCLK` / `H2`): under `2 ohm`.
   - Nu-Link pin 8 (`/RESET`) to pin 32 (`nRESET` / `H1`): under `2 ohm` when attached.
   - Nu-Link pin 10 (`VSS`) to pin 49 (`GND` / `H5` / USB shield): under `2 ohm`.
3. Measure resistance between adjacent signal nets in both probe polarities:
   - Pin 33 (`SWDIO`) to pin 34 (`SWCLK`) must not read near `0`-`2 ohm`.
   - Pin 32 (`nRESET`) to pin 33 and pin 34 must not read near `0`-`2 ohm`.
   - Pin 51 (`VDD` / `H4`) to pin 49 (`GND` / `H5`) must not read near `0`-`2 ohm`.
4. If pin 33 and pin 34 read `0 ohm` (observed during initial bring-up on 2026-11-13), lift both microprobe needles and measure the board pads and the free probe leads separately. If both are open when separated, the seated needles were bridging the adjacent legs.
5. Check `SP10` header jumpers: each `SP10` probe body exposes two header pins tied to the **same needle**. Use one jumper lead per probe unless intentionally splitting that signal to a passive observer such as a logic analyzer channel.

## Step 2: Rail ownership and power-up ordering (avoiding back-powering)

<Aside type="caution" title="Back-powering locks up Faultier with Windows error code 10">
Never allow a debug probe or an already-powered target rail to feed `3.3 V` into an unpowered Faultier crowbar or program header, and never allow the Nu-Link Pro to source target power while a Faultier crowbar lead is attached to `VDD`.
</Aside>

When a debug probe drives `3.3 V` onto the target `VDD` rail (`supply=1`) or the target is powered before Faultier USB enumerates, that `3.3 V` sits directly on Faultier's crowbar drain (`Crowbar D`) and any connected GPIO/sense lines. Current flows from the target `VDD` net backward through the RP2040 GPIO ESD protection diodes into the glitcher's internal `3V3` rail. Because the RP2040 core is partially energised through its I/O clamping diodes before its own USB regulator and power-on reset sequence run, the microcontroller enters a wedged state: Windows detects the USB device descriptors (`VID:PID` appears in Device Manager) but marks the composite device failed with **error code 10 (`This device cannot start`)**, and `usbipd` attachment produces no persistent device node.

Enforce three electrical rules across every session:

1. **Pick one rail owner.** Power the target board from its own USB cable (`VBUS` `4.92 V` feeding the onboard `3.3 V` regulator). Keep the Nu-Link Pro target-power output disabled (`supply=0`), and leave Faultier's `Program` header `3.3V/VCC` pin physically disconnected (`front view left-to-right: VCC, CLK, GND, IO, RST`).
2. **Keep one SWD master on `SWDIO`/`SWCLK`.** Attach either the Nu-Link Pro (`ICE_DAT`/`ICE_CLK`) or Faultier's `Program` header (`IO`/`CLK`), never both at once.
3. **Follow strict connect and teardown ordering.**
   - **Connect sequence.** (1) Plug Faultier into host USB first and confirm clean USB enumeration with all target leads disconnected; (2) plug in the Nu-Link Pro (with `supply=0`) and logic analyzer over USB; (3) wire the ground, SWD, sense, and crowbar leads to the unpowered target; (4) plug in the target mouse USB cable last to energise `VDD`.
   - **Teardown sequence.** (1) Unplug the target mouse USB cable first so `VDD` drops to `0 V`; (2) disconnect the harness leads from the target; (3) unplug the Nu-Link Pro and Faultier USB cables last.

With the target powered over USB and all tools idle, verify DC voltages with the multimeter in DC volts mode:

- Pin 51 (`VDD`) to ground: `~3.3 V` (Nu-Link pin 2 to pin 10 must match within a few millivolts).
- Pin 50 (`LDO_CAP`) to ground: `~1.26 V`. Never attach `VDD_SENSE` to pin 50; a `~1.26 V` or `~1.8 V` reading on the probe's voltage channel means the sense wire is on `LDO_CAP` or floating.

## Step 3: Nu-Link 3.3 V configuration and the safe-connect OpenOCD driver

OpenOCD can drive a Nu-Link Pro over USB HID, but it cannot configure the probe's programmable SWD I/O voltage translator, which defaults to `5 V` (`~4910`-`4930 mV` measured before configuration).

### Set 3.3 V I/O once in the official ICP tool

1. Open the Nuvoton NuMicro ICP Programming Tool (`3.24.8062r`)[^icptool] with the Nu-Link Pro connected over USB and the target disconnected.
2. Select **M480 Series**, click **Option** next to **Start**, set **Nu-Link Pro IO Voltage = 3.3 V**, and click **OK**.
3. Do **not** click **Start**, and do **not** accept any popup offering to unlock or mass-erase the chip (`Target flash is in security lock mode` -> cancel immediately).
4. Click **Stop check** if the tool is polling for a target, then **close the ICP tool completely** so it releases the USB HID handle before OpenOCD starts.

### Why the stock Nu-Link OpenOCD configuration is unsafe on a locked target

Inspecting OpenNuvoton's USB JTAG/SWD driver source (`src/jtag/drivers/`)[^openocdnuvoton] showed that stock adapter initialization performs `RESET_HW` and `RESET_SYSRESETREQ` and sends `CMD_SET_CONFIG` with `supply=1` during adapter open. On a target that is already USB-powered and wired to a crowbar harness, `supply=1` violates single-rail ownership, and unsolicited reset assertions disturb target state during a read-only check.

To prevent both hazards, apply a dedicated safe-connect HLA layout patch to xPack OpenOCD (`7bb05570f40dae2c742e6e581db4129468fed689`)[^xpack]:

1. Register a separate safe HLA layout so an unpatched OpenOCD binary fails closed before opening the USB device.
2. In the safe connect path, force `if_target_power_supplied(0)` (`supply=0`), send `CMD_MCU_RESET` in connect-only mode (`reset type 5` with `CONNECT_NORMAL`, powering the debug `AHB-AP` with zero pin or software reset), and reject explicit `SRST` assertions.
3. Expose a read-only `hla_command "raw_mdw <address>"` backed by the Nu-Link memory-read API so single 32-bit words can be queried without Cortex-M `CPUID` target examination.

```tcl
# Inline safe OpenOCD configuration
adapter driver hla
hla_vid_pid 0x0416 0x511c 0x0416 0x511d 0x0416 0x5200
transport select hla_swd
adapter speed 200
```

### The connect-only AP power-up trap: why skipping reset broke HLA memory reads

In the OpenNuvoton USB JTAG/SWD driver (`src/jtag/drivers/`)[^openocdnuvoton], adapter open calls the reset helper, which sends `CMD_MCU_RESET` bundling a hardware or system reset (`RESET_HW` or `RESET_SYSRESETREQ`) with `CONNECT_NORMAL`. When the initial version of the safe-connect patch skipped that `CMD_MCU_RESET` call altogether to avoid resetting the live mouse, it also skipped `CONNECT_NORMAL` - the command that tells the Nu-Link firmware to power up and initialise the target's Cortex-M Debug Access Port (`AHB-AP`). With the Debug Port (`DP`) awake (`DPIDR = 0x2ba01477`) but the `AHB-AP` never connected to the internal bus, every subsequent HLA memory read echoed the stale `DPIDR` word `0x2ba01477` from the adapter buffer.

Replacing the skipped call with a connect-only handshake (`CMD_MCU_RESET` using connect-only `reset type 5` and `CONNECT_NORMAL`, while keeping `supply=0` and suppressing all resets) powered the `AHB-AP` without resetting the chip. On the same locked board, OpenOCD then examined `Cortex-M4 r0p1`, read the real part ID from `SYS_PDID` (`0x40000000` = `0x00d48310`), and read live `SRAM` (`0x20000000`, initial stack pointer `0x20001c60`), while `APROM` (`0x00000000`), `LDROM` (`0x00100000`), and `SPROM` (`0x00200000`) returned `0xffffffff` under the flash lock. Note that `CONNECT_NORMAL` halts the running core on attach; resume or power-cycle the target after the read check completes.

### Fail-closed DPIDR check and the voltage-channel echo trap

Do not treat an OpenOCD exit code of `0` as target detection. When the SWD physical layer fails to get a response from the target, the Nu-Link USB driver buffer can retain the preceding voltage-sense response words and print them as the `IDCODE`:

| Recorded run state | Reported probe voltage | Returned `IDCODE` / `DPIDR` | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target disconnected (FW `7246`) | `3 mV` | `0x00030001` | Invalid: open sense line echo |
| Prior to ICP `3.3 V` setup | `4910 mV` / `4930 mV` | `0x0e381022` / `0x0e620ff7` | Invalid: `5 V` I/O mismatch |
| ICP set to `3.3 V`, open `SWCLK` | `3315 mV` | `0x0a4b0bde` | Invalid: broken `SWCLK` contact |
| Voltage-buffer echo (`06a9`, `06a4`) | `~1700 mV` (floating sense) | `0x06a406a9` | Invalid: raw voltage words echoed as `IDCODE` |
| Safe layout with open contact | `2963 mV` | `0x0b9f0b9f` | Invalid: `2963 mV` (`0x0b9f`) echoed twice |
| Safe layout after `SWCLK` repair | `2980 mV` (`200 kHz`) | `0x2ba01477` | **Pass**: valid Cortex-M4 `DPIDR` across 3 consecutive runs |

Notice `0x0b9f0b9f` in the fifth row: `0x0b9f` in decimal is `2975`, matching the `2963 mV` ADC sense reading. Always require an exact match on `0x2ba01477` before running any memory read or glitch pulse.
### Driving OpenOCD over Tcl RPC: run-control and memory read discipline

Once the safe-connect driver is loaded, every run-control and memory operation must go through OpenOCD's adapter-driven commands. Three rules that hold for Nu-Link HLA targets on the M480/M483 family:

Run-control goes through the adapter commands, never a raw DHCSR write. The Nu-Link firmware caches whether the core is halted or running and gates the `CMD_MCU_STOP_RUN` / `CMD_MCU_START_RUN` USB commands (which `halt` and `resume` proxy) against that cached state. Writing `C_HALT` or `C_DEBUGEN` directly to `DHCSR` (`0xE000EDF0`) through the memory-write path updates the real `DHCSR` but does not update the firmware's cached state. The firmware may then refuse to resume a core it believes is already running, or skip halting one it believes is already halted. The next memory transaction hangs OpenOCD's event loop indefinitely. The fix: use only `halt`, `resume`, `poll`, and `get_reg` through the Tcl RPC. These route through the firmware's native run-control commands and keep firmware state coherent with the core.

Drive OpenOCD over its Tcl RPC port, not stdin, and wrap reads in `capture { ... }`. OpenOCD ignores Tcl on stdin except in `gdb_port pipe` mode. When scripting OpenOCD programmatically, connect to the Tcl RPC port (configured with `tcl_port` in the config) over TCP, send Tcl commands terminated with `0x1A` framing, and read the framed response. Memory reads must be wrapped in `capture { ... }` so the result line is returned as the RPC result rather than emitted to the log stream. For a single word read:

```tcl
capture { mdw 0x40000000 1 }
```

Without `capture`, the `mdw` command returns empty through the RPC.

Call `poll` before `resume` after an HLA examine. After the HLA layer examines the target, OpenOCD leaves the target state `TARGET_UNKNOWN`. The `adapter_resume` command checks `target->state`, not the real `DHCSR` - so it refuses to resume from `TARGET_UNKNOWN` even when the core is visibly halted (`DHCSR.S_HALT` set). The sequence for a clean startup is:

```tcl
# After connect + examine:
poll      # Reads real DHCSR, updates target->state to TARGET_HALTED
resume    # Now works
```

All three rules are latent in any Tcl script that examines an HLA target and then attempts run-control. Upstream scripts avoid them by connecting, examining, and handing off to GDB without resuming.

### The connect-lockup: why a running locked core faults on debug attach

The companion reference, [Nuvoton M483 flash locks and the glitch-oracle trap](/reference/m483-flash-locks-and-glitch-oracles/#the-connect-lockup-attaching-to-a-running-locked-m480m483-core), covers the lockup mechanism in full. In summary: the safe-connect driver sends `CMD_MCU_RESET` with `CONNECT_NORMAL`, which tells the Nu-Link firmware to halt the running core as part of debug-connect. On a locked M483, that halt triggers a fault. The fault-handler vector reads from locked flash as `0xFFFFFFFF`, the core branches to `0xFFFFFFFE`, and the fault escalates to permanent LOCKUP (`PC=0xFFFFFFFE`, `xPSR` exception number `3`). Read the fault-status registers after the lockup to confirm: `HFSR` (`0xE000ED2C`) shows the `VECTTBL` bit (`0x40000000`), and `DFSR` (`0xE000ED30`) shows the `HALTED` bit (`0x00000001`).

The upstream Nuvoton OpenOCD source never hits this because it always resets the target before connecting - the core is stopped when `CONNECT_NORMAL` runs. With safe-connect (`RESET_NONE`, no reset), the core is still running, creating the untested code path.

A gentler connect mode exists in the Nu-Link protocol: `CONNECT_DISCONNECT` (enum value `4`). The upstream driver uses it in its close handler for clean disconnection, explicitly paired with `RESET_NONE`. Tested on the locked target, it does NOT power the `AHB-AP`: reads returned a garbage `IDCODE` (`0x0bbc0bc7`, not `0x2ba01477`) and `SRST` was rejected, so memory reads die. The Nu-Link bundles AP power-up with the halt in `CONNECT_NORMAL` and offers no power-without-halt path. There is therefore no non-perturbing debug attach on a running locked target through the Nu-Link; a raw-SWD probe (CMSIS-DAP) that powers the AP without the bundled halt is the remaining alternative.
## Step 4: Qualifying crowbar delivery with logic-analyzer and ADC baselines

Once SWD returns `0x2ba01477` and the crowbar is wired to `VDD` (pin 51) and `GND` (pin 49), verify that crowbar pulses actually reach the rail before running any timing sweep.

### Why onboard ADC misses nanosecond pulses

The glitcher calls `configure_adc(ADC_CROWBAR, 3000)` before firing `glitch()`. At the default clock divider of `1`, the RP2040 ADC samples at `250 MHz / 96 = 2.604 MS/s`, or one sample every `~384 ns`. A `4 ns` or `16 ns` crowbar pulse is `24x` to `96x` shorter than a single sample period: an unchanged ADC trace at `4 ns` proves that the ADC is undersampled, not that the MOSFET failed to fire. Time-resolving nanosecond pulses requires an oscilloscope; without one, use microsecond calibration pulses (`1 us` to `1 ms`) cross-checked by a `1`-`2 MHz` logic analyzer on `VDD` (`D1`) and `nRESET` (`D0`) to verify physical delivery and reset latency.

### LDO_CAP (pin 50) versus VDD (pin 51) calibration

On `LDO_CAP` (pin 50, `~1.26 V` DC with the factory `2.2 uF` decoupling capacitor installed), the normalised Faultier ADC baseline sits at `0.388`-`0.3917` (above `FaultierTool selfcheck`'s generic `0.15`-`0.30` open threshold, which assumes an unpowered pin). Pulses of `4 ns`, `16 ns`, `128 ns` (ADC min/max `0.3686`/`0.4392`), and `1 us` produced no resolved droop; a `10 us` pulse pulled the normalised ADC minimum down to `0.0235`.

Moving the crowbar drain to `VDD` (pin 51, `3.3 V` DC, normalised ADC baseline `0.919`-`0.933`) while leaving the `LDO_CAP` capacitor intact produced clean, repeatable rail pull-downs confirmed simultaneously by the onboard ADC and an `fx2lafw` logic analyzer:

| Programmed `VDD` pulse | Faultier ADC samples below `0.5` | Logic-analyzer `VDD` (`D1`) low width | Logic-analyzer `nRESET` (`D0`) response | USB PnP / SWD readback state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1 us` (`250` cycles) | Min `0.0392` (`3` low samples) | `~1 us` (`2` low samples at `2 MHz`) | High (`60,000,000` samples, `0` edges) | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `10 us` (`2,500` cycles) | `33` samples (span `488`-`521`) | `27` low samples (at `2 MHz`) | High (`60,000,000` samples, `0` edges) | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `25 us` | `79` samples | `32`-`33 us` (at `1 MHz`) | High | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `30 us` | `95` samples | Measured low (`1 MHz`) | High | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `35 us` | `111` samples | Measured low (`1 MHz`) | High | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `40 us` | `127` samples | Measured low (`1 MHz`) | **Low pulse** (`~35`-`37 us` after `VDD` fall) | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `45 us` | `143` samples | Measured low (`1 MHz`) | **Low pulse** (`~35`-`37 us` after `VDD` fall) | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `50 us` | `158` samples | `62 us` (at `1 MHz`) | **Low pulse** (`~35`-`37 us` after `VDD` fall) | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `100 us` | `306`-`307` samples | `119 us` (at `1 MHz`) | **Low pulse** | Unchanged / `locked-response` |
| `200 us` / `500 us` / `1 ms` | `637` / `1,741` / `2,983` samples | `247/670/1149 us` (at `1 MHz`) | **Low pulse** across all three rungs | Unchanged / `locked-response` |

Two hardware facts stand out from the ladder:

1. **External `nRESET` assertion starts between `35 us` and `40 us` of `VDD` pull-down**, with `nRESET` falling `35`-`37 us` after the `VDD` falling edge. Shorter pulses (`1 us` to `35 us`) droop the rail without triggering an externally visible pin reset.
2. **Every rung disarmed cleanly without dropping USB enumeration**, proving that the crowbar path delivers repeatable energy to pin 51 while keeping the peripheral alive.

## Gotchas and lessons learned

- **Back-powering through GPIO ESD diodes mimics a dead glitcher.** When Windows reported code 10 on Faultier's composite USB device, the hardware was undamaged: `3.3 V` on the crowbar drain was back-feeding the RP2040 `3V3` rail before USB enumeration. Enforcing a single rail owner (`supply=0` on Nu-Link, `Program VCC` disconnected on Faultier) and plugging Faultier USB in before target power eliminated the fault immediately.
- **OpenOCD exit status `0` is not target detection.** Stock scripts that only check whether `openocd` exited cleanly will pass when the target is completely disconnected, because the Nu-Link driver prints voltage-channel words (`0x06230625`, `0x0b9f0b9f`) as the `IDCODE`. Always parse the output log and assert `DPIDR == 0x2ba01477`.
- **Repeated `0x2ba01477` on memory reads is a dead-transport echo, not memory content or a lock signature.** Once `SWCLK` contact was repaired, reading Cortex-M `CPUID` (`0xe000ed00`), `CONFIG0`..`CONFIG3` (`0x00300000`), `APROM` (`0x00000000`), and peripheral registers via `raw_mdw` returned `0x2ba01477` at every address. Because `CPUID` returned `0x2ba01477`, OpenOCD decoded `PARTNO` as `0x147` and refused normal `mdw` commands with `Target not examined yet`. That happened because skipping `CMD_MCU_RESET` altogether on open also skipped `CONNECT_NORMAL`, leaving the `AHB-AP` unpowered so every HLA read echoed the `DPIDR` word. Switching the safe-connect driver to connect-only mode (`CMD_MCU_RESET` with `reset type 5` and `CONNECT_NORMAL`) powered the `AHB-AP` without resetting the chip: `SYS_PDID` (`0x40000000`) then read `0x00d48310`, `SRAM` (`0x20000000`) read live data (`0x20001c60`), and `APROM` still returned `0xffffffff`. Why that happens - and why you cannot run a fault-injection sweep until you have a positive control that beats it - is the subject of [Nuvoton M483 flash locks and the glitch-oracle trap](/reference/m483-flash-locks-and-glitch-oracles/).
- **Never move `VDD_SENSE` to `LDO_CAP` (pin 50).** Pin 50 sits right next to pin 51 (`VDD`), but it carries the `1.26 V` internal core regulator output. Connecting a probe's `VCC` sense pin to pin 50 produces a `~1.26`-`1.8 V` target-voltage reading and breaks `3.3 V` level translation.
